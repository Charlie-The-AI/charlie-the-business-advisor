# Glossary of Terms

Clear explanations of technical and business terms used throughout Charlie.

---

### Average Order Value (AOV)
The average dollar or currency amount spent each time a customer places a completed order:
$$\text{AOV} = \frac{\text{Net Revenue}}{\text{Completed Orders Count}}$$

### Gini Inequality Coefficient
A statistical measure of distribution inequality ranging from `0.0` (perfect equality across all catalog items) to `1.0` (extreme concentration where a single product drives 100% of revenue).

### Grounding Verification
The automated verification process Charlie performs on all AI-generated text. Ensures that every numerical statistic, percentage, or currency figure cited by an LLM matches the deterministic analytics calculations in the snapshot.

### Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)
A standard economic metric measuring market concentration, calculated as the sum of squared revenue percentage shares of all products. Scores above 2500 indicate high revenue concentration risk.

### Minor Currency Units
The smallest subdivision of a currency stored as an integer (e.g. cents for USD/EUR, kobo for NGN). Eliminates binary floating-point rounding errors.

### Net Recognized Revenue
True top-line sales recognized by the business after subtracting line-item discounts, promotional coupons, order cancellations, and refunds.

### Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)
The observation that roughly 80% of business consequences (e.g. sales revenue) stem from 20% of causes (e.g. vital catalog products or top VIP customers).

### Recency, Frequency, Monetary (RFM)
A customer segmentation methodology that evaluates how recently a customer purchased (R), how often they purchase (F), and how much money they spend (M).

### Vital Few vs Long Tail
In Pareto analysis, the top contributors that cumulatively account for the first 80% of revenue are called the **Vital Few**. The remaining catalog items that produce the remaining 20% are the **Long Tail**.
